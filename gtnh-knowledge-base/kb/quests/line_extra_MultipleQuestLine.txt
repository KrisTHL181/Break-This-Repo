All recipes have 3 or more outputs, and every output has a chance to appear: the chance for each is 100% divided by the number of outputs. To increase it, there are additional mechanics that can be used.  Simply put, the QFT uses huge amounts of energy and quantum field stabilization to disassemble simple molecules and assemble them into more complex ones, without needing additives or constrained processes. The only addition to the base inputs that the multi requires is a specific catalyst, which the smart circuits use to guide the materials into what they ultimately transform into, but they are never consumed or lost in any way.  This direct process simplifies long processing chains to one step, but it has two consequences: it makes the process expensive, given the extreme conditions needed to undergo the transformation (in a similar way to fusion), but it also generates many different outputs at once, none of which are guaranteed, and it's impossible to choose between them without adding something else into the setup to alter the conditions inside the QFT in some way. ### [2209] Moron's Guide to Breeding Better Bees
First off, don't be scared by the massive amounts of bees you see here once you unlock the breeding trees. There's even more that aren't included and you'll need NEI to find! The active one affects the behavior of the bee while the inactive one is only relevant for breeding.  Every trait further either [warn]Dominant[/warn] or §9Recessive§r, normally respectively colored red and blue. When getting newly bred princesses and drones, check both sets in case you got the desired traits. You can breed the ones with inactive traits to try and make them active in later crosses. A Field Kit or GT scanner cannot see passive traits, you need a Beealyzer for that.  When breeding bees, a good idea is to settle upon a master template bee - a bee that has little to no crossbreeding potential but has all the traits you want - short life, nocturnal, cave dwelling, rain tolerant, humidity tolerance, temperature tolerance, 4x fertility, and so on. Once you have that bee, breed a bunch of pure princesses with those traits as both active and inactive. These will be your breeding stock.  When you are trying to get new species, what you want is a drone - this way you can use your "breeding stock" princess with the drone to get new species without the annoying effects or limitations. Poorly-crossed princesses can be returned to normal by crossing with breeding stock drones, or just thrown into storage if they now have annoying restrictions. Make sure you keep the trait and species you want, and slowly merge the good traits from your breeding stock. You don't have to do this part in an Apiary, you can use a Bee House to swap around traits. Remember that no species mutations occur in a Bee House. Ignoble bees are good to swap traits because they're mostly disposable - they have a chance to die after 100 breeding cycles, increasing slightly more each generation after that, though genetic stability frames can let them live longer.  Once you solidify your species, create a pool of drones. You can use those to convert pristine princesses to the new species. Pristine princesses will not die, but they can become Ignoble under certain circumstances like using tons of production frames. Rocky bees are a great source - all their princesses are always pristine, they have decent traits, and there's a bunch of them underground. If you have an ender quarry, you can collect them with that.  Turning two different species traits into a third species trait is a §omutation§r. Merely turning one species into another is not. The chance of this happening is shown in the bee breeding tab, as well as the conditions. Bees can require things like blocks underneath their structure, certain moon phases, or other things nearby in order for the mutation to happen at all. Mutations happen across Active-Inactive and Inactive-Active pairs. So if you have an A:B princess that you want to turn into a C:C princess, you want to use an A:B drone. This crosses the princess' active A with the drone's inactive B, and the princess' inactive B with the drone's active A. This gives a shot at mutation for both the active and inactive traits for the resulting princess and drones.  A new bee mutant is considered "pure" or "purebred" when both the active and inactive traits are the new species. This is what you need to complete the breeding quests, but also to retain that species indefinitely. If the mutated trait only appears in one trait, the chance of it carrying is basic cross-breeding and not whatever was required to cause the trait to mutate in the first place. New bee species also have a set of default traits, and the new species will try to manifest these traits. You could start with two species that have compatible climate conditions and wind up with one needing completely different temperature and humidity. You want to encourage traits as you finish breeding the pure bee to get the other traits you want out of it.   Ultimately, the species trait determines what the bee produces and everything else is fair game. All traits can be bred into any bee species, aside from the species itself. Species also determines the initial preferred temperature and humidity. Species require their preferred temperature and humidity to be in the 'jubilant' state and produce their special drops. You can also mimic this with the alveary or industrial apiary, but increasing tolerances will NOT work.  Advanced frames can provide useful boosts to mutation chances or lifespan reduction for faster breeding cycles. However, the excessive multipliers will increase genetic decay, giving a chance for a pristine princess to become ignoble.  A recommended early-game meta for bee breeding is to find an Oblivion Frame in a Stronghold library, and then a means to keep it repaired like with a Thaumic Restorer. This rapidly speeds up the turnaround time to turn a queen into offspring. You monitor the process manually at an apiary, and use any additional frames you can to improve the mutation rate. You can push this well past the point you can make alvearies, but there are alveary components that can further improve on this.  It looks complex, because it is! ### [2161] §c§lSafety First
For those new to GregTech, here are some tips and warnings about electricity.  Machines don't like being out in the rain, and will start fires nearby. Fires next to a machine have a random chance of an explosion. Also, sending a higher voltage than a machine's tier causes an explosion. Or several, if you pack things together, like many people. Sending more amps than a machine can use doesn't harm anything though.  Sending too high a voltage or too many amps down a cable will cause the cable to burn up and start fires, which may then cause machine explosions. Make sure the number of amps provided by generators and buffers to a cable does not exceed the amps it is capable of carrying.  A generator that supplies 1A can possibly feed multiple machines, if their recipes require less than the full voltage. For example, macerators processing ore only need 2 EU/t, so you could run several from a single 1A generator, as long as the wires aren't too long. Why does that matter? Because there's loss for every amp for every block, listed on the wire/cable. Note that cables are better than wires. But be careful! Sometimes a recipe needs a lot more EU than you expect, like a macerator sand -> quartz dust recipe.  Most machines only use 1A, however there are exceptions, such as the thermal centrifuge, world accelerators, and the UUM machines. Note that machines can accept more amps than they use (usually 2) in order to top up their energy supply, especially if they didn't receive most of the amp because of loss (mostly a problem in LV). The formula is (EU/t of Recipe * 2) / Voltage Tier + 1. Keep this in mind when deciding on cables.  Some machines are specifically there to help you deal with multiple amps, such as battery buffers, transformers, and diodes. Check out the quest tab Storing and Transforming EU for more on those.  If you want to send more amps, use a larger cable or a superconductor (MV+, but ULV and LV have their own lossless cables). You can use laser hatches much later to send even more. There even are wireless options, but not right now.  A single block machine starved of EU will restart progress but not lose the recipe items. A multiblock machine will lose the items.  Note that any block that emits EU has loss when doing so: 2 ^ (tier-1) per amp. So 1 EU at LV, 2 EU at MV, and so on. Using large numbers of battery buffers in a row is thus very wasteful of your power. You have to be careful with losses when making your first EBF later in LV, maybe rather power them directly? Of course, if you want to keep your amps topped up over longer distances, you'll need to place BBs occasionally to 'top up' the amps, though this is less the case the further ingame you get, as the loss becomes less of something to worry about.  If this info isn't enough for you, head on over to [url]https://wiki.gtnewhorizons.com/wiki/Electricity[/url] to read more.  If you prefer to learn via video there are also great YouTube resources such as HamCorp's Electricity Guide: [url]https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lpDPY8J-4QY[/url]
